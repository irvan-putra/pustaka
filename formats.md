# Two formats for the gathering

Both formats keep literature at the center and stay open to people who use AI, people who avoid it, and people still deciding. Neither requires slides, preparation, or any tool use from attendees.

## Format 1: Casual discussion (default)

The simplest version: a facilitated open discussion with no program. A suggested length is two to three hours.

One way to run it:

- A host opens with the theme and one ground rule: every position on AI, including rejection, is welcome.
- Discussion moves through a few prompts; people speak from their own reading and writing life, not from expertise.
- No demos, no presentations, no homework.

Suggested prompts:

- How do you actually read and write these days, and has that changed recently?
- When a machine helps write something, how does that change how you feel about the result?
- What part of reading or writing do you most want to keep human, and why?
- What would make you trust, or stop trusting, a piece of writing?

## Format 2: Show-and-tell plus discussion (optional)

A more structured variant for a community of builders:

- Short show-and-tells (5-10 minutes each) where people demo something they built that serves readers or writers. Failed experiments are explicitly welcome; what did not work is often the most useful part.
- Then an open discussion on authorship, consent, and trust: who wrote this, who is allowed to use it, and how do readers know?

Notes:

- Demos are about serving reading and writing, not about promoting a tool.
- Keep the same ground rule: questioning the tools, including the ones just demoed, is part of the event.

## Choosing between them

A suggested starting point is Format 1: it needs no speakers, no equipment, and no preparation from attendees, and it suits any accessible space where a group can sit and talk. Format 2 is for when a community has projects to show and wants a more program-like event; it can grow out of a casual gathering later rather than replace it.

The timings and formats above are untested suggestions, not proven recipes.
