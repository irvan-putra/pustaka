# pustaka

A meetup concept: a casual public gathering about reading, writing, and books, with or without AI.

**Status: concept only.** No venue, date, organizer, or speaker is attached to this repository, and nothing here is a commitment by anyone. It is a format anyone can pick up and adapt.

## The idea

Public library event listings show that similar conversations about AI and readers and writers have been held. This concept is one way to run such a gathering:

- **Literature is the theme.** The whole gathering is about reading, writing, and books. AI is one thread of the conversation, not the headline.
- **The public can join.** An open meetup, not a closed community session.
- **No AI or coding required.** Nobody is asked to use any tool to take part.
- **Skepticism is welcome.** Questioning or rejecting AI tools is a legitimate position in the discussion, not a view to be argued out of the room.

The design bar: the gathering should be worth attending for someone who wants nothing to do with AI.

## Formats

Two formats are sketched in [formats.md](formats.md):

1. **Casual discussion** (the default): no slides, no formal talks, just a facilitated open discussion.
2. **Show-and-tell plus discussion** (optional, more structured): short demos of projects that serve readers or writers, including what failed, followed by an open discussion on authorship, consent, and trust.

## Public precedents

Public library event listings show similar events exist (checked 16 November 2026):

- Coquitlam Public Library (Canada) lists an event titled "AI and the Future of Storytelling", a panel of local authors on AI's impact on readers, writers, and publishers: <https://coquitlam.libnet.info/event/17091816>
- The New York Society Library lists "New Tools, New Questions: Introducing Artificial Intelligence for Readers and Writers", a workshop series: <https://www.nysoclib.org/events/AI-workshop-new-tools-new-questions-introducing-artificial-intelligence>

These are examples that the genre exists, not endorsements or affiliations.

## Adapting this concept

If you run a version of this:

- Keep the event open to the public and keep literature at the center.
- Make clear in the invitation that no AI use is expected or required.
- Do not list speakers, venues, or dates as commitments before every side has confirmed them in writing.
- If a co-organizer or speaker shares something privately while planning, keep it out of public material until they agree to publish it.
